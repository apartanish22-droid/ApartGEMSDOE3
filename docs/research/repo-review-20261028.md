## Candidate hypotheses to preregister (not implemented or validated)

These are research proposals only. They have not been tested in this review; novelty is based on a code-level scan of `src/gems32/`, not an exhaustive history of every sibling repository. Expected gains are qualitative priorities, **not score projections**. All rely on the existing numerical feature stack, but need a proper per-feature leakage canary and spatially blocked holdout before any submission build.

| Priority | Hypothesis / layer(s) | Targeted physical signature | Why it may find uncatalogued structures | Difference from present code / cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cross-spectral phase-consistent basement discontinuity:** GeoDAWN magnetic RTP/TMI (bands 2/14) and gravity anomaly / vertical gradient (bands 13/11), at multiple upward continuations | Localized cross-wavelet coherence and phase rotation across scale bands, constrained to linear edges whose strike persists across depth | A deep fault can juxtapose density and susceptibility contrasts while lacking a surface scarp; a phase-consistent, cross-field edge could reject single-channel lithologic contacts and line noise | Existing code computes gravity/magnetic curvature, tilt and upward continuation, but no identified shared cross-spectral phase-coherence operator in the scan. Low-to-medium implementation; moderate expected lift, high geophysical attribution risk |
| 2 | **Hydrochemical residual corridors:** GDR #1391 spring chemistry/temperature (silica, chloride, Na/K where measurements exist) plus mapped volcanic vents; condition on elevation and chemistry completeness | Robust residual anomalies in reservoir geothermometer / mixing indicators, spatially connected to independent geophysical lineaments | Hot, chemically evolved upflow can indicate blind permeability not represented by mapped Quaternary faults; chemistry provides an independent heat/flow process rather than another topographic surrogate | Current code has GDR vent/well proximity and geothermometry, but a measurement-quality-aware multivariate chemistry residualization was not identified in the scan. Medium implementation; likely sparse data and high spatial clustering risk. Free source already mirrored: GDR #1391; provenance and license should be rechecked before production |
| 3 | **Structural relay topology from existing deformation bands:** GeoDAWN geodetic shear/dilation rates (bands 7–8), seismicity (bands 10/16), with gravity/magnetic lineament geometry | Graph-based stepovers, pull-apart relay basins and strain-localized nodes, using orientation-compatible line intersections rather than pixelwise score addition | Hidden faults are likely to occur at accommodation zones and fault tips, which a fault catalogue records incompletely; these geometries emphasize new segment termini rather than tracing mapped lines | Existing modules use strain, seismicity, tip/relay cones and CELF; the distinct part would be an explicit uncertainty-weighted graph/topology representation. Medium-to-high cost; expected lift uncertain, strongest uniqueness risk versus prior lane artifacts |
| 4 | **Independent geologic-contact negative control:** USGS State Geologic Map Compilation (SGMC) map-unit boundaries / lithology polygons (official USGS source) alongside DEM and magnetic/gravity edges | Boundary-conditioned edge veto and within-unit robust contrast; test whether candidate lineaments persist across map contacts | Helps distinguish fault edges from lithologic contacts and volcanic-caprock boundaries that mimic fault scarps; can improve precision on off-catalogue structures | Existing checks use an SGMC fault diagnostic, not a complete map-unit boundary confounder model.
